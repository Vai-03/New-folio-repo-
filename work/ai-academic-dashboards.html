Overview

What it is

A 0→1 B2B2C platform for universities in the GCC, with an AI “aspiration engine” at its core. One product serves students planning careers, staff running daily operations and admins steering the institution.

As the sole product designer, I shaped an AI aspiration engine alongside persona-specific dashboards—recommendations, goals, and progress without forcing every user into the same generic home.

The problem

Why it needed design

Universities had data on students but little that helped a student decide what to do next, and staff juggled several disconnected tools.

A single generic dashboard would have failed everyone: the three groups have very different jobs, permissions and attention spans.

The design question: how might one platform feel personal to each role while staying one coherent product with one design system?

Users

Who we designed for

Proto-personas built from the product’s user roles and early conversations.

L

Layla

Second-year undergraduate

“I know I should be planning, I just don’t know where to start.”

Goal: Turn vague ambitions into concrete next steps

Frustration: Generic advice; no view of her own progress

O

Dr. Omar

Academic advisor and faculty member

“I have 200 advisees and ten minutes for each.”

Goal: Spot who needs help early and act quickly

Frustration: Data spread across systems; manual reporting

S

Sara

University administrator

“Leadership wants outcomes, not activity counts.”

Goal: Institution-wide view of engagement and outcomes

Frustration: Inconsistent reports; no trusted single number

Research

What we found

Qualitative

  • 01Students trusted recommendations more when they could see why they were suggested.
  • 02Staff wanted fewer, sharper alerts rather than more charts.
  • 03Admins needed exports and consistent definitions they could take into leadership meetings.
  • 04Arabic and English bilingual use shaped layout, density and labelling decisions from the start.

Reflection

What I learned

01

Explainability is a feature. AI recommendations landed better when the “why” was one tap away.

02

Shared structure, different emphasis: the same layout grammar can feel personal to very different users.

03

Being the sole designer means designing the system as well as the screens, so the team can keep building without you.
