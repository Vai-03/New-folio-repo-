Overview

What it is

An inspection and maintenance tool for oil and gas plant workers, with fast handheld capture in the field and a planning dashboard for supervisors, connected through the whole shift.

Legacy tools assumed a desk and mouse. I split fast field capture from supervisor oversight, keeping evidence, routing, and close-out connected through the full shift.

The problem

Why it needed design

Legacy inspection tools assumed a desk and a mouse; plant workers use them with gloves, in glare, while being interrupted.

Findings captured in the field often lost context by the time they reached a supervisor, slowing decisions on risky assets.

The design question: how might we make capture fast in the field and oversight clear at the desk, without breaking the chain between them?

Research

What we found

Qualitative

  • 01Technicians are frequently interrupted, so every step had to save progress automatically.
  • 02Photos and voice notes carried more information than typed text in the field.
  • 03Supervisors trusted AI risk predictions more when confidence and history were shown.
  • 04Shift handoffs were where context was most often lost.

Reflection

What I learned

01

Design for the environment, not the desk: gloves, glare and noise are hard constraints.

02

Auto-save and resumable flows matter more than speed when interruptions are constant.

03

AI predictions earn trust when they show their confidence and their evidence.
